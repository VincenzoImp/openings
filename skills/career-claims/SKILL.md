# Career claims

Read the owner request, current Openings Profile, the Writing rules of the
owner's Preferences, relevant Career descriptions, reusable claims and selected
template guidance and limits. Use Globish: clear international
English with precise technical terms. Clarity, factual accuracy and reader value
come before a controlled vocabulary.

## Build the strongest supported account

Read the relevant experience descriptions and linked Projects together before
deciding what is missing. Connect existing evidence into a coherent account of
the person's work: the problems addressed, engineering choices, transferable
strengths and value for the actual opportunity. Do not limit positioning to
verbatim bank claims when the current document rules allow supported new wording.

Use reasonable synthesis to make demonstrated capabilities explicit. Distinguish
that synthesis from a new factual claim: related tools or parallel code do not
alone establish deployment across machines, measured speedups or sole ownership.
Ask a focused question when the answer could materially strengthen positioning
or resolve a consequential uncertainty, after checking the existing evidence.

Choose the strongest relevant examples and explain why they matter to the
employer. Keep factual limits in the supporting context; include them in public
wording when needed for accuracy, rather than filling the narrative with generic
caveats that obscure the contribution.

Application documents sell the owner's strongest truthful case. Never volunteer
weaknesses: do not name a missing requirement, a lack of experience, something
"new to" the owner, a "transition" or years the owner lacks. Where the record does
not cover a requirement, lead with the closest demonstrated, transferable work and
leave the rest unmentioned. Never claim unsupported experience either. Internal
caveats belong in a note on the application. Answer a direct form question truthfully.

When maintaining Career records, integrate new information into the
relevant existing passages, reconciling it with the full history, chronology and
contribution scope. Replace superseded wording and remove repetition instead of
appending dated clarification blocks. Keep source and verification notes in
metadata; retain dates that describe the work itself. Document-only preparation
still follows the application-copy boundary below.

## Build the statement

Explain what the person did, which relevant methods or tools they used, and why
the work matters. A supported capability or purpose is useful without a measured
improvement. Keep concrete mechanisms instead of generic promises of efficiency.

Separate personal actions, team actions, system functions and study findings.
Preserve dates, system versions, contribution boundaries and completion status.
Owner statements are evidence within their stated scope. Code alone does not
prove authorship, production use or measured performance.

Use a quantity only with its meaning, unit, source and attribution. Dataset size
is not an improvement, a target is not a measurement, and a paper's finding is
not automatically one person's contribution. Keep uncertainty explicit when it
matters. Do not fabricate a baseline, saving, adoption figure or metric.

Keep the full context sufficient to explain the problem, personal work, method,
results and limits. A concise bullet need not carry every supporting detail.
Use distinct contributions rather than synonym-only duplicates. Do not present
one continuing project as separate achievements in multiple CV sections.

## Apply the document contract

The Openings rules define fixed fields, permitted changes, slot counts and
Skills selection: the Writing rules of Preferences (`writing`, scope `all`, or
`cv` or `letter` for that document) and the template's guidance and limits. Claim selection is typed: start a CV from the
claims with `default_in_cv`, prefer `role: core` over `supporting`, take a
`bench` claim only when the job calls for it, and avoid two claims of one
`group` saying the same thing. Use approved claims when they make the case well. When the
rules allow it, write new wording from the relevant frozen Career evidence.
Keep each contribution under its correct role and period. A related project can
clarify that role only where the relationship and contribution are recorded.

Store application-specific wording in the document, with its evidence links.
Do not automatically save it as an official Career claim or change MASTER.
Use real claim IDs for the claims actually reused; new wording needs no invented
claim ID. Follow the [Openings workflow](../openings-workspace/SKILL.md).

## Check meaning and presentation

Use natural sentences in descriptions, letters and answers. CV action bullets
may omit the first-person subject, and inline entries may use concise phrases.
Keep official titles, names, identifiers, quotations and Skills terms accurate.

Use the actual template font and frame. Follow its line preferences, preserve
material meaning and fix dangling words. Occupancy targets are guidance, not
instructions to add filler. Do not shrink the document to conceal overflow.
Inspect the complete PDF and extracted text before storage.

The [STE reference](references/asd-ste100.md) and
[technical-term register](references/technical-terms.md) are optional tools for a
specific wording question. Do not force an STE substitution that changes meaning
or naturalness, and do not claim certification. The register is not a personal
Skills inventory. See [Writing principles](references/writing-principles.md)
for examples and public source guidance.
