# Evidence and source guidance

Use Globish by default for prose and claims. Keep precise technical English
when useful. The [STE reference](asd-ste100.md) offers optional editing checks.
Use these sources for claim structure and reader relevance.
They do not replace the STE dictionary or the owner's document rules.

## Source to sentence

These examples are writing exercises. They are not claims about the owner.

| Source evidence | Writing decision |
|---|---|
| A collector records download outcomes and source identifiers. | Show how analysts can separate missing pages from retrieval failures. Keep the collection method. |
| An app sends prepared images to a server and shows returned matches. | Give the capture-to-result sequence. Do not invent accuracy, class counts or adoption. |
| Several workers use durable progress records. | Show how interrupted work can start again. Do not invent a runtime reduction. |
| A person's analysis caused a team response. | Keep the person's analysis separate from the team's corrective actions. |

Keep the method and supported purpose together.
A generic promise of efficiency does not replace implementation detail.

## Use of the sources

- Google gives an accomplishment, measurement and method approach.
  Use real measurements. Do not make a percentage for each bullet.
- Headless Headhunter gives a recruiter's view of purpose and job relevance.
  Keyword quotas and interview-ratio promises are the author's heuristics, not universal rules.
- ETH gives competence and application guidance.
  Its general CV format does not override the owner's template or Openings rules.
- Keep distinct claims and sufficient source context.
  Do not turn each fact into a separate CV sentence.

The owner request and the owner's Writing rules in Openings control the document.
These sources do not authorize new facts, submission or fixed-template changes.
