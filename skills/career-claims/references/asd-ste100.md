# Optional STE reference for CV claims

The source is [ASD-STE100 Issue 9](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf), dated 2025-01-15.
Use Part 1 with the Part 2 dictionary. Do not copy the standard into Git.
A client can read an owner-supplied or lawfully obtained copy outside Git.
PDF page numbers below refer to that 434-page edition.

## Local profile and limits

The owner selected Globish for prose, contexts, letters and stored instructions.
This means clear international English, not a controlled 1,500-word vocabulary.
Globish is also the default for claims and accomplishment bullets. Keep precise
technical English when useful. STE is an optional editing resource, not a
preferred register or a hard vocabulary, sentence-length or approval gate.
The rules below explain STE. They are not a lexical gate for all Career writing.
CV action bullets can omit the first-person subject and use a past-tense action
lead. An inline CV entry can use a concise noun phrase.
These exceptions concern CV sentence form. They do not approve every verb.
Prose uses complete clauses. Instructions use direct sentences.
Within STE itself, descriptions use full sentences and procedures use imperatives.

Keep official titles, names, quotations, code identifiers and structured display
fields accurate. Use applicable technical-noun categories for those items.
Do not call this profile certified, automatically compliant or fully strict STE.
If a required template format conflicts with a rule, record the conflict.
Do not silently change a fact or expand the exception.

## Rule map for manual checks

The standard has 53 writing rules in nine sections.
This map does not replace their explanations or the dictionary.

|---|---|---|
| 1.1–1.14 | Select dictionary words, technical nouns or technical verbs. Examine meaning, part of speech, forms, category and spelling. Keep one name for one item. | 45–62 |
| 2.1–2.2 | Keep noun groups short. Explain an established longer technical name with a short form or valid grouping. | 63–66 |
| 3.1–3.7 | Examine permitted verb forms and tenses. Use active voice. Examine participles and each `-ing` use. Prefer a direct approved action verb. | 67–76 |
| 4.1–4.5 | Write clear sentences without omitted words or contractions. Use lists, connecting words and necessary articles. Apply the CV exception only to its stated scope. | 77–86 |
| 5.1–5.5 | In procedures, use sentences of at most 20 words. Separate instructions unless actions occur together. Keep notes descriptive. | 87–94 |
| 6.1–6.6 | Give descriptive information in a logical sequence. Use at most 25 words per sentence and six sentences per paragraph. Keep one topic per paragraph. | 95–102 |
| 7.1–7.3 | Apply the safety rules when an actual safety instruction is necessary. State the risk accurately. Do not invent a safety warning for ordinary career prose. | 103–106 |

The CV profile includes subject omission and concise inline noun phrases.
These are explicit departures from full-sentence rules, including 3.6 and 4.2.
Its sentence-length checks apply only when assessing STE, not as mandatory CV limits.
A visual line and a grammatical sentence are different units.

## Dictionary procedure

1. Find the word in Part 2.
2. Examine the entry's part of speech, meaning, forms and help notes.
3. If necessary, select an accurate alternative or change the sentence structure.
4. For a domain term, examine rule 1.5 or 1.12 and the term register.
5. Make sure that the new sentence keeps the source meaning and attribution.

The dictionary introduction is on PDF pages 131–148.
An uppercase dictionary entry is approved only for its stated use.
Capitalization of a word in a draft does not establish approval.
A lowercase entry is not a general approval. Its alternatives are suggestions.
Technical-noun approval does not give unrestricted verb approval.
Technical verbs must also obey section 3.
Use a precise approved alternative before a technical-verb exception when it
communicates the same meaning (rule 1.12, PDF page 59).

## Checks that prevent false substitutions

- `CHECK` and `TEST` are approved nouns. General verbs *check* and *test* require
  another construction or a separately justified technical use.
- *Build* is not a general approved verb. Its physical-assembly alternative does
  not automatically describe software work.
- *Develop* and *implement* are not unrestricted dictionary-approved verbs.
  Examine the actual computer/scientific process before a technical-verb use.
- `RECEIVE` has a restricted energy/material/signal meaning. Do not use that
  approval for receiving an award.
- A statistical *accuracy* value and *precision* are different quantities.
  Keep the correct technical metric instead of a misleading replacement.

Record the entry or rule and the reason for a technical use.
A count script or spelling scan can find possible problems.
It cannot prove meaning, part of speech, technical-term eligibility or full compliance.
