# Writing CVs, letters and highlights

The owner's Writing rules and the template guidance come first; this is the
default method under them.

## The strongest true case

- Write only what the career entries or the owner support. Reasonable
  synthesis is fine ("built and ran the data pipeline" from an entry that
  describes both); a new fact is not: no invented metric, tool, scale, team
  size, production use or sole ownership.
- Lead with the work closest to the role's needs and say why it matters to this
  employer. Use the employer's terms for skills the owner really has.
- Never volunteer a weakness: no missing requirement, "new to", "transition"
  or "although I lack". Where the record does not cover a need, lead with the
  nearest real work and leave the rest out; the gap goes in a note for the
  owner. A direct form question is answered truthfully.
- Keep personal work, team work and what a system does apart: "I designed",
  "our team shipped", "the service handles".
- A number needs its unit, period and source. A target is not a result;
  dataset size is not an improvement.
- One project is one achievement: do not repeat it under several headings.

## CV

- Start from the template and the ★ highlights; swap in the approved
  highlights that fit the role; drop the least relevant before shrinking
  fonts or margins.
- New wording for this job is allowed when the Writing rules allow it; it stays
  in this document, cites its career entries and does not become a highlight.
- Bullets start with a strong past-tense verb, name the method or tool and the
  result or purpose, and fit the template's bullet limits.
- Keep titles, employers, dates and degrees exactly as recorded.

## Letter

- Three short paragraphs unless the rules say otherwise: why this role and
  employer (specific, from the posting); two or three pieces of evidence
  matched to their main needs; a plain close.
- Address a named person only when the posting names one.
- No generic praise of the company, no clichés ("passionate", "dynamic",
  "team player"), no repetition of the CV line by line.

## Plain international English

Most readers are not native speakers and many screen in a hurry.

- Short sentences, one idea each; active voice; common words.
- Precise technical terms are welcome: name the tool, protocol or method
  exactly ("PostgreSQL", "Kafka", "gradient boosting") rather than a vague
  synonym. Spell product names as their makers do.
- One term per concept throughout the documents.
- No idioms, sports or culture-bound metaphors, or abbreviations a recruiter
  may not know; expand an acronym on first use unless it is standard in the
  field (API, SQL).
- Use the posting's language when it asks for one and the owner writes it;
  otherwise English, with the spelling the Writing rules set.

## Highlights

A highlight is one reusable line about one contribution: action, method,
result or purpose, in under about 25 words, true on its own. Save new ones as
not approved; mark one approved only when the owner approves that wording.

## Before you upload

Read the PDF as the employer will: page count, layout, links, names, dates,
spelling, and every claim against the record and Facts about me.
